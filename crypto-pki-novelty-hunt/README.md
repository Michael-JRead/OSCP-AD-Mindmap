# Crypto / PKI Novelty Hunt — constant provenance, key-leakage bugs, CA software, app-server state (2026-10-23)

Prompted by three questions: *could we find something like a CA private-key compromise, a PHP2Shell-class
bug elsewhere, or a discovery like October 2026's NIST curve-seed result?* Seven hunters, each with an
independent skeptic. All work was local, on public constants and on keys we generated ourselves.

**Scope note:** we did not attempt to obtain any real third party's private key — no CT-log / scan-data
weak-key mining, no harvesting of real signatures, no contact with real CA or ACME endpoints. The real
"private keys exposed" incidents (Debian OpenSSL, ROCA, PuTTY P-521, the "Ps and Qs" duplicates) all came
from **implementation bugs**, so that is what we hunted. PHP itself stayed out of scope during the
PHP2Shell exploitation wave.

> **Disclosure status:** 5 confirmed vulnerabilities (1 High, 1 Medium, 1 Moderate, 2 Low), all
> **unpatched**; details are embargoed and go to the maintainers first. The constant-provenance results
> below have no security impact and are published in full.

## 1. NIST curve-seed provenance (research, publishable)

**Background.** On 2026-10-16 Anthropic reported that the NSA-supplied seeds of NIST P-192…P-521 were produced
by `lrand48` seeded with Unix timestamps from 1997–98, that four NIST binary-curve seeds matched too, and that
the **B-163** seed did not ([primary post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins);
coverage: [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/17/wow-it-s-a-real-discovery-claude-cracks-a-25-year-nsa-number-mystery)).

**Independent reproduction.** All 14 explained seeds were regenerated byte-for-byte against OpenSSL master's
`ec_curve.c`, and — separately — re-derived **from the seeds alone** (no timestamps given) with our own
exact-rational LLL + Babai nearest-plane recovery of the `lrand48` state, stepping the LCG back to the
`srand48` form. Every timestamp and candidate index matches the published table.

**B-163 — still unexplained (rigorous negative).** Seed `85E25BFE5C86226CDB12016F7553F9D0E693A268`, identical in
three independent transcriptions, and self-authenticating (it reproduces `b` when SHA-1 output is read in the
Gaussian normal basis, as for B-233/B-283/B-409). Hypotheses rejected:

| Search | Scale | Hits |
|---|---|---|
| Seeding-agnostic lattice fingerprint (recovers internal state from the seed itself, so valid at any seeding and stream depth): 11 LCG/PRNG families incl. `lrand48`/`drand48`, `java.util.Random`, ANSI/BSD/MSVC/Borland `rand`, minstd, MMIX, FreeLIP; every output slice, bit offset, byte/bit order, stride 1–3, 32-bit composition idioms | 185,268 tests (expected chance hits 2.9e-6); positive controls 7,590/7,590 | 0 |
| Direct seeded search: glibc `lrand48`/`random()`, 4.3BSD `random()`, `rand` variants, MIRACL `irand`/`brand` and default state; 18 non-linear compositions incl. 1 bit/call | ~5.9 × 10⁹ (seed, position) pairs; generators match glibc 2.39 exactly | 0 |
| SHA-1 families: relations to other seeds/constants, phrase+counter, FIPS 186 App. 3.1 generator with small/timestamp XKEY | ~4.65 × 10⁶ evaluations | 0 |

Open gaps for anyone continuing: high-bit 1-bit-per-call slices needing lattice dimension ≥ 60, the whole
1997–99 seed era for `random()`/MIRACL beyond our 1-core budget, X9.17/X9.31 DES generators and secret-XKEY
FIPS 186 (untestable without the key), Mersenne Twister, and 1997-era MIRACL source fidelity.

**Clustering all 38 seeded curves:** cluster A — `srand48(t)`, 20 bytes `lrand48()>>23` (the 10 NIST prime
curves); cluster B — `srand48(t/2)`, five plain `lrand48()` words (four B-curves); cluster C — the 22
Certicom SEC 2 / X9.62 example seeds plus sect163r1, which contain the ASCII string "MinghuaQu" at varying
nibble offsets; **B-163 fits none of them.**

## 2. Other constants (research, publishable)

- **RFC 5114 DH groups (new, exactly re-runnable):** all three generators equal `2^((p-1)/q) mod p` — the
  FIPS 186-3 A.2.1 "unverifiable" generator with the first candidate `h = 2` (h=2 is the only base in 2..4095
  that hits; chance under the null ≲ 2^-139 per group even with a 2^20 look-elsewhere factor). So `g` carries
  no freedom; `p` and `q` remain unexplained (no seed published, none in the NIST CAVS PQG vectors).
- **Certicom SEC 2 / X9.62 seeds (minor, new structural observation):** after removing "MinghuaQu", three seed
  pairs share 17–21-nibble residual strings with an edited prefix byte, a final-nibble change and a moved name
  (chance ≤ 6e-16) — i.e. hand-edited variants of a few base strings, not independent random draws. A simple
  "increment the last nibble until the curve passes" hypothesis was tested and refuted.
- **Negatives:** no hash-of-structured-input origin found for SM2, FRP256v1, wapip192v1, GOST CryptoPro-B/C and
  TC26 parameters, Dual_EC's Q (provenance hypotheses only — no discrete-log work), or RFC 5114's q, across 9
  hash functions (incl. SM3) and ~53k structured inputs with exact mod-p matching. Brainpool's RFC 5639
  derivation reproduces exactly (sanity check). GOST "first prime admitting a CM curve" derivations reproduce
  arithmetically but were judged not statistically significant enough to claim.

## Takeaways
1. **The B-163 seed is a genuinely open problem** — and now a well-mapped one: large families of plausible
   1990s generators are ruled out with positive controls, so the remaining hypothesis space is much smaller.
2. **"Private key compromise" research is implementation research.** No properly generated CA key is
   recoverable; key leaks come from nonce generation, RNG handling and fault handling — which is where the
   confirmed findings were.
3. **Trust boundaries that look internal aren't.** The highest-severity finding of this round treats data from
   a remote server as if it were trusted local configuration — the same "check here, use there" pattern as
   every earlier round, just across a network boundary.
